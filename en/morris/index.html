What is Nine Men's Morris?

Nine Men's Morris is one of the oldest board games in the world, played for more than 2,000 years. Two players share one board of 24 points, joined by lines. Each player has nine pieces — one side black, the other white — and the goal is simple: line up three of your pieces to take your opponent's pieces, until your opponent has almost nothing left.

The game has two parts. First you place your pieces, one at a time, on empty points. Then, once all the pieces are on the board, you slide them along the lines to neighboring points. It sounds simple, but every placement matters: a piece put down carelessly in the opening can be hard to defend later.

How to play

  1. First, place your pieces. Take turns putting one piece on an empty point. Black moves first. Keep going until all nine pieces of each side are on the board.
  2. Make a mill to take a piece. When three of your pieces line up on a connected line, you have made a mill, and you may remove one of your opponent's pieces from the board. You cannot take a piece that is inside a mill — unless every one of your opponent's pieces is in a mill.
  3. Then move your pieces. Once everything is placed, slide one piece at a time to a neighboring empty point along a line. A mill made by moving also lets you take one of your opponent's pieces.
  4. Down to three pieces? You can fly. When you have only three pieces left, you are no longer limited to neighboring points — you can fly to any empty point on the board.
  5. How to win. You win by reducing your opponent to two pieces, or by blocking them so they have no move left. If the same position comes up three times, or if no mill is made for 50 moves, the game ends in a draw automatically.

Two versions: classic Morris and Morabaraba

Classic Nine Men's Morris is played with nine pieces each, on the board of 24 points described above.

Morabaraba is the 12-piece version of the game. Each player has twelve pieces, and the board gains four extra diagonal lines. Pieces can move diagonally too, and three in a row on a diagonal also makes a mill. It plays faster and more openly than the classic game, and it rewards sharp eyes for diagonal lines.

Both versions are available in the same game. You can switch between them whenever you start a new game.
